# India Evidence Audit: Navigation Copilot for Public-Service and Education Interfaces

## Bottom line

The pitch has a strong evidence base, but several tempting claims must be narrowed. There is **no nationally representative measure directly showing the share of all Indians unable to independently complete an online-government task**; the defensible substitutes are task-specific ICT capability, household use of e-government, and observed reliance on outside assistance. Likewise, CSC transactions prove enormous assisted-service demand, but do **not** identify unique citizens, inability, or universal out-of-pocket payment.

## Corrections to assumptions

### Digital inability

Do not say “75.3% of Indians cannot complete a government task” by simply taking the complement of 24.7% computer literacy. Computer literacy is a self-reported general capability measure; government-task completion depends on device, language, portal design, documents, authentication, connectivity, and procedural knowledge. For a pitch, say: **“India lacks a national task-completion benchmark, but official surveys show only 18.1% of rural people aged 15+ were computer literate in 2020–21, and only 21% of rural 15–24-year-olds could combine search, email and online banking in 2022–23.”**[^3][^2]

### CSC demand

Do not call 48.54 crore CSC transactions “48.54 crore citizens who could not self-serve” or “48.54 crore paid visits.” Transactions can repeat the same person, include financial/commercial activity, and may involve services chosen for convenience rather than inability. The safe slide language is: **“The assisted-digital channel processed 48.54 crore transactions in FY 2025–26, showing service demand at population scale.”**[^7]

CSC fee and burden evidence is credible but local and dated. Label ₹47 plus ₹20 travel as a **Maharashtra study benchmark**, not a current national average; use the Jharkhand evidence to show variance and risk of overcharging, not to price a national market.[^9][^10]

### EdTech size and dropout

Market-sizing firms use incompatible boundaries—content only, software, hardware/smart classrooms, K–12, test prep, higher education, or all learning technology—so attach the source and definition to every TAM figure. The US$7.5B-to-US$29B IAMAI–Grant Thornton estimate is usable if presented as **one industry estimate**, while government-platform scale should use SWAYAM’s 5.80 crore cumulative enrolments.[^17][^13]

Do not label SWAYAM’s roughly 9.1% certification/enrolment ratio a 90.9% dropout rate. Enrolments are cumulative, users can enroll in multiple courses, certification may require optional paid/proctored exams, and the published aggregates do not form a learner-level completion cohort.[^17]

### Learning science

The 0.47 SD assumption is exact: Freeman et al. synthesized 225 undergraduate STEM studies, while the exam/concept-inventory effect itself came from 158 studies. Avoid claiming it proves the effect of this particular copilot: it supports the **mechanism** of active participation over lecture, not product efficacy, Indian public-service navigation, or AI tutoring.[^23][^24]

Retrieval practice and spacing should be split into two claims. Practice testing versus restudy is approximately g = 0.51; spaced versus massed retrieval is g = 0.74 after publication-bias correction. These comparator and context differences mean the effects must not be added together or translated directly into percentage-point product gains.[^25][^27]

### Language barrier

The 2011 Census figure does not establish comfort, literacy, or ability to complete digital tasks in English. Lead instead with the current behavior measure: **57% of urban internet users preferred Indic-language content in 2024**; then cite BHASHINI as infrastructure evidence, not proof that translation alone resolves procedural or usability barriers.[^32][^29]

### Support-offset scale

The National Consumer Helpline provides a clean, official workload indicator but covers consumer grievances, not all public services. The commercial US$3.86B contact-centre estimate includes offshore/private-sector work and should appear only as a broad adjacency, never as directly addressable government-support spend.[^38][^36]

## Recommended pitch wording

- **Problem:** “Official capability data show that only 21% of rural Indians aged 15–24 could search online, use email and transact through online banking as a combined skill set in 2022–23.”[^3]
- **Behavioral proof:** “India’s CSC network processed 48.54 crore assisted-service transactions in FY 2025–26; the figure measures transactions, not unique digitally excluded citizens.”[^7]
- **Learning mechanism:** “Across 225 undergraduate STEM studies, active learning improved assessed performance by 0.47 standard deviations; this validates the learn-by-doing principle, not this product’s outcome.”[^23]
- **Language:** “In 2024, 57% of urban internet users preferred Indic-language content, while BHASHINI now serves 22 Indian languages at multi-billion-API-call scale.”[^29][^32]
- **Validation plan:** Run an India-specific randomized task study measuring independent completion, time, error rate, help requests, calibrated confidence, retention after 7–30 days, and safe escalation to humans. This is necessary because no national source currently measures the product’s precise target outcome.

## Strongest opening statistic

> **“Only 21% of rural Indians aged 15–24 could do all three: search the internet, use email, and complete online banking.”** — MoSPI, Comprehensive Annual Modular Survey 2022–23.[^4][^3]

This is the strongest opener because it is recent, official, rural, task-oriented, and directly connected to independent digital navigation. Phrase it exactly as a **combined ICT-skill measure**, not as the share that can complete a government service.
